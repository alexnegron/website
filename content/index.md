I am a mathematical scientist pursuing my PhD in the [Program in Applied and Computational Mathematics (PACM)](https://www.pacm.princeton.edu/) at Princeton University. I'm advised by [Boris Hanin](https://boris-hanin.github.io) and work closely with [Gautam Reddy](https://reddylab.physics.princeton.edu). I'm grateful to be supported by the [Department of Energy Computational Science Graduate Fellowship (DOE CSGF)](https://www.krellinst.org/csgf/). 

I think about the following question: 
> *What makes **life** and **mind** different from matter and energy?*

Mathematics sings a poem about matter and energy (physics) but has relatively little to say about life and mind. The sweet (bitter?) irony is, of course, that life and mind are made of matter and energy. 

I think about these ideas in a few settings: 
- mathematical foundations of deep learning theory 
- statistical mechanics of complex biological systems 
- theoretical neuroscience

I get excited about mathematics in the convex hull of the following areas: 
- interacting particle systems, probability theory 
- random matrix theory 
- statistical mechanics of disordered systems
- high-dimensional phenomena in probability and statistics
